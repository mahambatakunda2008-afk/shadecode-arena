# Shadecode Arena beta and prize-distribution plan

Status: proposed operating plan. A beta is for product and commercial validation, not permission to run a regulated prize competition.

## 1. Naming decision

- **Platform:** Shadecode Arena (also referred to as SC Arena).
- **Game working title:** Shade Tiles: Rush.
- **Retired public game name:** Tile Rush, because an existing game uses that identity and creates naming and discoverability conflict.
- **Other game:** Circuit Breaker.
- **Status:** Shade Tiles: Rush is a provisional working title, not a trademark, domain, app-store or legal clearance. Complete clearance checks before public branding and store submission.

Keep internal engine/package identifiers stable during this transition where changing them would create needless breakage. Public-facing copy, store metadata, screenshots and marketing should use the cleared game name consistently.

## 2. What the beta must prove

The beta should answer whether Arena can attract and retain players, create repeat competition, and generate real commercial demand before substantial spending or money features are built.

Run the beta in measurable stages:

### Closed product beta
- Invite a small cohort of real players across different devices and connection conditions.
- Test onboarding, game clarity, session completion, crashes, fairness, replay verification, leaderboard integrity and player support.
- Use free play and non-prize leaderboards only.
- Gather structured feedback and observe repeat use, not just sign-ups.

### Open community beta
- Expand only after critical reliability and fairness issues are resolved.
- Run scheduled free seasons, daily challenges and community competitions.
- Test referrals, social sharing, event participation and retention.
- Keep prize-bearing events disabled until the exact format has passed legal and payment review.

### Commercial validation alongside the beta
Test willingness to pay without collecting player entry fees or implying that a cash competition is live:
- Interview schools, clubs, communities, brands and event organisers.
- Present clearly scoped tournament-hosting and sponsorship packages.
- Seek written expressions of interest, pilot agreements and sponsor commitments.
- Test cosmetic and optional membership concepts with non-binding research before building checkout.
- Track qualified leads, conversion to paid organisation pilots, sponsor renewal intent, support costs and gross-margin estimates.

A survey response or verbal enthusiasm is not revenue. Stronger evidence is a signed agreement, confirmed budget, paid fixed-fee service that has been independently cleared, or a sponsor contract with funds received. Do not accept or hold prize funds during the beta.

## 3. Beta scorecard

Measure by cohort and acquisition channel:
- Activation: invited users who complete onboarding and their first game.
- Engagement: weekly active players, games per active player and completed sessions.
- Retention: day-1, day-7 and day-30 return rates.
- Competition: event joins, completion rate, repeat participation and leaderboard disputes.
- Quality: crash-free sessions, latency, failed games, replay verification failures and support response time.
- Trust: suspected cheating, confirmed abuse, moderation cases and privacy complaints.
- Commercial: qualified sponsor leads, organisation leads, proposals, signed pilots, cash actually received for cleared services, delivery cost and contribution margin.

Do not use downloads, registrations, social impressions or hypothetical prize-pool interest as proof of a viable business on their own.

## 4. Prize distribution should scale with the field

When prize competitions are legally cleared and fully funded, prizes should not automatically go only to first place. The number of paid places should scale with the number of eligible, verified participants and the published event rules.

A sensible starting policy for design and modelling is:

| Verified eligible field | Suggested paid places |
|---:|---:|
| 2–19 | Top 3 only when the field is large enough to support meaningful awards; otherwise top 1–2 |
| 20–49 | Top 3 |
| 50–99 | Top 5 |
| 100–249 | Top 10 |
| 250–999 | Top 10–25, depending on the published event format |
| 1,000+ | Top 25–100 or a defined percentage, such as the top 5–10% |

These are planning bands, not fixed promises. The final number of places must be published before registration closes and must not change after results are known.

### Allocation rules

- The event defines a fixed, fully funded prize budget before it opens.
- The rules state the number of paid places, exact award per place or percentage schedule, tie-breaks and treatment of tied ranks.
- The total allocation cannot exceed the funded prize budget.
- If a tie spans prize positions, apply a published tie policy. A common approach is to pool the affected position amounts and split them equally, rather than silently breaking ties after the event.
- Only server-verified eligible results count. Suspicious results can be held for review under published deadlines.
- No prize is advertised until its funding is confirmed and the format is legally cleared.
- Keep prize money separate from Arena revenue and operating cash.

### Illustrative percentage schedules

For modelling only, not an adopted payout promise:
- **Top 3:** 50% / 30% / 20%.
- **Top 5:** 35% / 25% / 18% / 13% / 9%.
- **Top 10:** 25% / 16% / 12% / 10% / 8% / 7% / 6% / 6% / 5% / 5%.
- **Top 100:** use a published tiered schedule or a flat minimum award with a clearly stated top-place premium. Model payment fees and payout administration before choosing it.

Every schedule must total exactly 100%. The event should choose its schedule before launch based on field size, funding and payout costs. Do not imply that a top-100 payout is sustainable for a small field or small prize pool.

## 5. Fairness and safety requirements

- No paid boosts, extra moves, retries, score multipliers or preferential matchmaking.
- Server-authoritative event timing, scoring and replay verification.
- Clear eligibility, age rules, anti-cheat review and appeals process.
- No entry fees, player-funded pools, cash-equivalent prizes, wallets or payouts during the initial beta.
- Sponsor-funded and organisation-funded prize events are also disabled until their specific legal review is complete.
- No sale of personal player data. Sponsor reporting should be aggregated and privacy-conscious.

## 6. Decision gate after beta

Do not move to a money-enabled competition pilot because the app has downloads or because a prize pool attracts attention. Require evidence across four gates:

1. **Product:** players return and complete competitions; reliability and fairness are acceptable.
2. **Commercial:** sponsors or organisations demonstrate budget-backed demand, not just likes or survey intent.
3. **Legal and payments:** qualified Zimbabwean advice, required approvals, and written provider acceptance for the exact entry, prize and settlement mechanics.
4. **Operations:** verified results, moderation, disputes, refunds, accounting, prize-liability controls and settlement reconciliation are ready.

If one gate fails, continue free play and improve the model. Funding alone does not clear legal or operational restrictions.
