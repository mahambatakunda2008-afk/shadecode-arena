# Circuit Breaker

Circuit Breaker is Arena's native logic-puzzle game, designed around spatial reasoning rather than Tile Rush's match-three loop.

## Core loop
- A seeded 5×5 board connects a lower-left source to an upper-right target, then adds staged objectives as chapters advance.
- Tap a tile to rotate its wire clockwise. Power travels only across reciprocal ports.
- Every board is generated from a seed and has a guaranteed intended solution.
- The route is generated by a seeded, bounded self-avoiding search between the fixed endpoints. It can include non-monotonic detours and is longer than the shortest route when a suitable path is found; a guaranteed shortest-path fallback keeps generation reliable.
- The unused cells contain single-ended decoy wires. These add visual noise but cannot bridge two cells or create an alternate source-to-target route.
- Later levels increase the minimum scramble depth: one rotation at levels 1–3, two at levels 4–7, and three from level 8. This is a starting difficulty curve and must be calibrated with playtests.
- Chapter 2 begins at level 5 and introduces fixed blocker tiles in off-route cells. Blockers cannot be rotated and never occupy the guaranteed route. From level 10, the generator places up to three blockers.
- From level 7, each campaign board also includes a route switch. The wire path can be aligned while the circuit remains unpowered; the player must explicitly activate the switch to complete the level. Switch activation is a move and is included in the move count. Daily mode remains a fixed level-4 puzzle without blockers, switches or checkpoints.
- Chapter 3 adds ordered, latched checkpoints: one from level 9 and two from level 12. The player must connect the source to each checkpoint in sequence before the target can complete the circuit. A checkpoint remains secured once reached, even if a later rotation temporarily breaks that section of the network. The UI labels checkpoint tiles and displays secured progress.
- Chapter 5 introduces a fixed crossover bridge from level 21. The main route passes straight through one channel while the perpendicular channel remains electrically isolated. The bridge displays four ports but power entering vertically can only leave vertically, and power entering horizontally can only leave horizontally. The tile is fixed and cannot be rotated. Connectivity traversal tracks the entry side at each bridge, preventing power leakage between channels.
- Chapter 4 introduces required side branches from level 15: one branch terminal from level 15 and two from level 18. Each branch is a one-port terminal connected to a three-way junction on the main route. The player must power every branch terminal as well as secure the ordered checkpoints and reach the target. Branch terminals are included in the guaranteed solution and receive their own wire scramble.
- A hint identifies a rotatable route tile and the clockwise rotations needed to restore its intended orientation. Decoys, endpoints and fixed crossover bridges are deliberately excluded from hints.
- Completion unlocks the next level. The level strip follows the most recently unlocked levels so players can revisit progress beyond level five.
- A daily challenge uses a date-derived seed and a fixed difficulty, so players receive the same offline puzzle for their local calendar date. Daily completion is recorded separately from campaign progression.
- Best move counts, unlocked level, completed-level history and recent daily completions are saved locally with AsyncStorage. Gameplay requires no network, timer, entry fee or competitive reward.

## Multi-network rules foundation
- The rules engine exports `CircuitNetwork` and `evaluateCircuitNetworks`. Networks can declare their board width and owned cell indices; BFS traversal is bounded to the declared route, so one network cannot borrow another network's path.
- A `createMultiNetworkPuzzle(seed, networkCount)` generator produces a 7×7 board with two, three or four independently powered, differently coloured, non-overlapping routes. The default remains two networks for backwards compatibility; the native challenge screen exposes all three counts. Each route is generated with seeded bounded self-avoiding DFS, so route topology and length vary rather than merely reflecting one fixed path. The generator protects the other network's endpoints and occupied route cells, retries bounded route pairs, and retains a known disjoint fallback pair. Wire scrambles and decoy orientations are also seeded. Both networks have fixed source and target endpoints and their own solution masks. `rotateMultiNetworkCell` updates completion only when both destinations are powered.
- The native dual-grid screen is `apps/mobile/app/circuit-breaker-multi.tsx` and is linked from the original Circuit Breaker screen. It shows per-network power status, colour-coded routes, individual connected-cell counts, move count and a new-board action. The original single-network campaign remains unchanged.
- Two-network routes now use bounded randomized self-avoiding path generation between fixed endpoint pairs. The generator retries route pairs to preserve disjoint ownership and falls back to a known valid pair if search budgets are exhausted. Generated crossover mechanics and campaign integration remain future work. Three- and four-network modes use separated endpoint lanes and a guaranteed disjoint fallback when bounded randomized search cannot find a route set. The dual-grid engine is available on its own native screen, linked from the original Circuit Breaker screen, but is not yet integrated into campaign progression.

## Technical design
- Pure TypeScript rules and generation live in `apps/mobile/src/circuit-breaker.ts`.
- The seeded generator searches for a self-avoiding route with a bounded DFS budget and an 18-cell cap, reserving board space for later branch objectives. It falls back to a guaranteed shortest path if needed, assigns reciprocal wire ports, adds single-port decoys and level-gated fixed blockers, and scrambles route tiles.
- Blockers are represented as non-conductive cells. Rotation requests against them are ignored, and connection search cannot traverse them.
- The route switch is puzzle-level state, separate from wire-port masks. Connection detection remains disabled until the switch is activated; activation re-evaluates the existing board and can complete the puzzle immediately.
- Checkpoint progression is puzzle-level state. A source-origin BFS identifies currently connected cells; ordered checkpoint indices are latched as they become reachable. The target only completes the puzzle after all checkpoints have been secured and the switch is active. This preserves progress when a player later rotates a previously aligned tile.
- Side branches extend the solution graph without changing the main source-to-target route. A branch junction receives an additional reciprocal port and its terminal receives the matching single port. Completion requires all branch terminals to be in the source-connected component. Branch terminals are included in the route-cell mask so they are scrambled, hinted and covered by the seed regression suite.
- Connection detection uses breadth-first search and requires reciprocal ports between adjacent tiles. Traversal state includes the entry port so crossover bridges preserve two independent channels rather than treating all four ports as one junction.
- The native screen is `apps/mobile/app/circuit-breaker.tsx`; it is linked from Arena home.
- Progress is device-local. It is not synced across devices and is not a ranked score.

## Known limitations and next validation
- The route search is intentionally bounded and may fall back to a shortest path for some seeds. Routes remain limited to a 5×5 board. The current campaign layers switches, ordered checkpoints, required side branches and isolated crossover bridges onto one main source-to-target network. A seeded 7×7 dual-network generator and solver now exist in the pure rules engine, with regression coverage across 256 seeds of randomized two-network route pairs, plus 64 seeds each for three- and four-network boards, including full-solve, route ownership and adjacency checks. Route generation is bounded and uses a known disjoint fallback; three/four-network levels and physical-device playtesting remain outstanding. Switches are implemented from level 7, ordered checkpoints from level 9, required side branches from level 15 and isolated crossover bridges from level 21.
- Single-ended decoys are visual distractions, not yet interactive hazards or complex branching wire networks.
- Scramble depth is not a calibrated difficulty rating. Playtest move counts, solve times and hint frequency across a broad seed sample.
- A native CI regression suite exercises 960 deterministic boards across levels 1–24, restoring every intended route and checking checkpoint, branch, switch, blocker, bridge and endpoint invariants. Additional fixtures verify that crossover channels remain isolated, route ownership prevents leakage at shared ordinary junctions, independent networks cannot borrow power from one another, invalid route ownership fails closed, and fixed bridges are never suggested as rotatable hint targets. It runs before Expo exports.
- Validate glyph orientation, touch targets, screen-reader announcements, persistence after force-close and small-screen layout on physical Android and iOS devices.
- The daily puzzle currently uses the device local date and device-local completion record. A future cross-region daily competition would need an agreed timezone, signed seed schedule and server-side validation.
- Do not add paid entry or prizes to this offline puzzle mode without a separately reviewed competitive format and jurisdictional clearance.
